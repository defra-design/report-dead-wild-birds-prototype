Report dead wild birds

How to report dead wild birds

Use this service to report dead wild birds that may have died from disease as this helps us understand how diseases are spreading.

Public reports of dead wild birds help us to track diseases and identify appropriate responses.

Reports help explain where bird flu is in Great Britain and in which types of wild birds.

Not all dead wild birds have been infected with bird flu.

Do not use this service to report

  • sick and injured birds
  • birds where the cause of death is known (that have not died from disease, for example run over by a car or flown into a window)
  • a small number of garden birds and pigeons — please use Report Online - Garden Wildlife Health for this

Do use this service if, at the same time, you find

  • a single dead bird of prey, swan, goose, duck, gull (or blackbird in Scotland only)
  • five or more dead wild birds of any other species

We may collect and test reported birds for bird flu or other diseases.

If you’re under 18, ask a parent or guardian to fill in this form.

When you submit a report, we’ll ask for your contact details.

You can also report dead wild birds by phone on 03459 33 55 77 (find out about call charges).

{{ govukWarningText({ text: "Do not touch dead birds with bare hands", iconFallbackText: "Warning" }) }}

If you want to know how to dispose of dead wild birds or ask for them to be removed, read the guidance on removing and disposing of dead wild birds.

If you have found a dead wild bird and suspect it has died as a result of:

  • wildlife crime, you must report them to the police
  • poisoning from pesticides or rodenticides, call the Wildlife Incident Investigation Scheme (WISS) on 0800 321 600 (calls are free)

If you’ve found a sick or injured bird, follow the guidance for reporting other dead, sick or injured animals.
