Report dead wild birds

This service is to report dead wild birds for disease investigation.

What to report

You should report:

  • 1 or more dead birds of prey, swans, geese, ducks, seabirds or, in Scotland, any blackbirds
  • 5 or more dead wild birds of any other species found in one area at the same time

What not to report

Do not report:

  • living birds that are sick or injured
  • 4 or fewer dead birds, except for the species already named

These birds will not be helpful for this monitoring programme so we will not collect them. There are other ways to deal with these dead birds.

Other services that help with dead and injured birds

If you’ve found a sick or injured bird that is still alive, follow the RSPB’s guidance on what to do.

If you want to know how to dispose of dead wild birds on your property or ask for them to be removed from public land, read the guidance on removing and disposing of dead wild birds.

{{ govukWarningText({ text: "Do not touch dead birds with bare hands.", iconFallbackText: "Warning" }) }}

If you’ve found a dead wild bird and suspect it has died as a result of:

  • wildlife crime, you should report it to the police
  • poisoning from pesticides or rodenticides, contact the Wildlife Incident Investigation Scheme (WISS)

If you’ve found any birds you think are sick or dead from disease (excluding waterfowl, waders, gulls and birds of prey), you can send a ‘disease incident report’ to the Garden Wildlife Health reporting service.

What we do

We decide which eligible dead birds to collect based on their number, species, location and likely condition. We may test those we collect for bird flu or other diseases.

If you have trouble using the online form, call Defra’s help centre so someone can submit the details for you.
