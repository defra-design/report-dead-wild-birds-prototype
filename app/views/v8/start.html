Report dead wild birds

What to report

You should only use this service to report birds that may have died from disease. You should report:

  • any dead birds of prey, swans, geese, ducks, gulls or, in Scotland, any blackbirds
  • 5 or more dead wild birds of any other species found in the same area at the same time

What not to report

Do not report:

  • living birds that are sick or injured
  • birds that have died from injury (for example run over by a car or flown into a window)
  • 4 or fewer dead birds, except for the species already mentioned

If you’re under 18, ask a parent or guardian to fill in this form.

What we do

We decide which dead birds to collect based on their location and likely condition. We test those we collect for bird flu or other diseases. Your report may help us understand how diseases are spreading.

If you believe a bird has symptoms of a disease but does not meet the criteria for reporting, telephone us on 03459 33 55 77 (find out about call charges).

Other services that help with dead and injured birds

If you want to know how to dispose of dead wild birds or ask for them to be removed, read the guidance on removing and disposing of dead wild birds.

If you’ve found a dead wild bird and suspect it has died as a result of:

  • wildlife crime, you must report it to the police
  • poisoning from pesticides or rodenticides, call the Wildlife Incident Investigation Scheme (WISS) on 0800 321 600 (calls are free)

If you’ve found a sick or injured bird that is still alive, follow the RSPB’s guidance on what to do.

If you’ve found a small number of dead garden birds or pigeons, you can report them through the Garden Wildlife Health reporting service.
